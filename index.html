Engineering · Thermal systems

Keeping data centres cool, one loop at a time

6 min read  ·  Northstar Learning

Modern processors turn a surprising amount of electricity into heat. If that heat stays trapped, chips slow down to protect themselves — and the computers we rely on become less efficient.

A heat exchanger transfers thermal energy from one fluid to another without letting the two fluids mix. In a liquid cooling loop, warm water picks up heat near the processor, then releases it as it passes through a separate cooling circuit.
Context Vela is using · paragraph 2

Because liquid carries heat more effectively than air, these systems can use less energy while keeping dense computing equipment at a stable temperature.

What is a heat exchanger?
It’s a device that moves heat from one fluid to another without mixing them. Here, warm water carries heat away from the processor and passes it to a separate cooling circuit — like two lanes exchanging warmth through a shared wall.
